# M01 Notes

### 1. General Probability & Moments

**Cumulative Distribution Function (CDF):**
The probability that a random variable \( X \) is less than or equal to \( x \) [1]:
\[ F(x) = \text{Prob}(X < x) = \int_{-\infty}^{x} p(x') dx' \]
\[ \text{Prob}(a < X < b) = F(b) - F(a) \]

**Change of Variable:**
If \( x \rightarrow y = y(x) \), the probability density preserves the normalization condition [2]:
\[ g(y) = \frac{p(x)}{|dy/dx|} \]
**Pitfall Flag:** Always remember the absolute value for the derivative \( |dy/dx| \) to ensure the probability density remains positive, and take extra care if \( y(x) \) has critical points [2].

**Expectations & Moments:**
The mean \( \mu \) is the first moment [3]:
\[ \mu \equiv \text{E}[X] = \int_{-\infty}^{\infty} x p(x) dx \quad \text{or} \quad \sum_{k} x_k p(x_k) \]

**Variance (\( \sigma^2 \)) Derivation:**
Variance is the dispersion based on the second moment [4, 5]. Using the linearity of the expectation operator [6]:
\[ \sigma^2 = \text{Var}(X) \equiv \text{E}[(X - \mu)^2] \]
\[ \sigma^2 = \text{E}[X^2 - 2\mu X + \mu^2] \]
\[ \sigma^2 = \text{E}[X^2] - 2\mu\text{E}[X] + \mu^2 \]
\[ \sigma^2 = \text{E}[X^2] - \text{E}[X]^2 \]

**Higher Moments (Shape Characteristics):**
*   **Skewness (Asymmetry):** Dimensionless normalized third cumulant [5].
    \[ s \equiv \frac{\text{E}[(X - \mu)^3]}{\sigma^3} = \text{E}\left[ \left(\frac{X - \mu}{\sigma}\right)^3 \right] \]
*   **Kurtosis (Tail Weights):** Based on the fourth moment. The standard formula subtracts 3 so that a Gaussian distribution has a kurtosis of exactly zero [5].
    \[ \kappa \equiv \frac{\text{E}[(X - \mu)^4]}{\sigma^4} - 3 \]
**Pitfall Flag:** Do not assume moments always exist. "Fat-tailed" distributions, like the Cauchy distribution \( p(x) = \frac{A}{\pi(A^2 + x^2)} \), have infinite variance and their higher moments diverge, meaning the Central Limit Theorem (CLT) may not hold [7].

### 2. Covariance, Correlation, & Portfolios

**Covariance & Correlation:**
\[ \text{Cov}(X,Y) \equiv \text{E}[(X - \mu_x)(Y - \mu_y)] = \text{E}[XY] - \mu_x \mu_y \]
\[ \rho(X,Y) = \text{Corr}(X,Y) \equiv \frac{\text{Cov}(X,Y)}{\sqrt{\text{Var}(X)\text{Var}(Y)}} \]
**Pitfall Flag:** If variables are independent, their covariance is zero; however, the converse is mathematically false. A vanishing covariance (\( \text{Cov}(X,Y) = 0 \)) does *not* imply independence [8, 9].

**Portfolio Returns:**
For a portfolio of \( N \) assets with weights \( w_i \) [10, 11]:
\[ R_p = \sum_{i=1}^{N} w_i R_i \]
\[ \mu_p = \text{E}[R_p] = \sum_{i=1}^{N} w_i \mu_i \]
\[ \sigma_p^2 = \text{Var}(R_p) = \sum_{i=1}^{N} w_i^2 \sigma_i^2 + 2 \sum_{i < j}^{N} w_i w_j \sigma_i \sigma_j \rho_{ij} \]

### 3. Cheat Sheet of Common Distributions

**1. Uniform Distribution** [12, 13]
*   **Density:** \( p(x) = 1 \) for \( x \in [14] \)
*   **Mean:** \( \mu = \frac{1}{2} \)
*   **Variance:** \( \sigma^2 = \frac{1}{12} \)

**2. Binomial Distribution** [10, 15, 16]
*   **Probability:** \( f(k; n, p) = \binom{n}{k} p^k q^{n-k} \) where \( q = 1 - p \).
*   **Mean:** \( \mu = np \)
*   **Variance:** \( \sigma^2 = npq \)
**Pitfall Flag:** When coding numerical calculations, evaluating the factorial \( n! \) directly will quickly overflow machine limits; always use built-in functions like `dbinom` or `choose` [11].

**3. Poisson Distribution** (Limit of Binomial as \( n \rightarrow \infty, np = \lambda \)) [17-19]
*   **Probability:** \( p(k; \lambda) = \frac{e^{-\lambda} \lambda^k}{k!} \)
*   **Mean:** \( \mu = \lambda \)
*   **Variance:** \( \sigma^2 = \lambda \)

**4. Gaussian (Normal) Distribution** [13, 15, 20]
*   **Density:** \( p(x) = \frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi\sigma^2}} e^{-(x-\mu)^2 / 2\sigma^2} \)
*   **Standardized Variable:** \( z = \frac{x - \mu}{\sigma} \), yielding \( p(z) = \frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}} e^{-z^2/2} \)

**5. Lognormal Distribution** [21, 22]
*   **Density:** \( g(y) = \frac{1}{y\sqrt{2\pi\sigma^2}} e^{-(\log y - \mu)^2 / (2\sigma^2)} \)
*   **Mean of return:** \( \text{E}[R] = e^{\mu + \sigma^2/2} - 1 \approx \mu + \sigma^2/2 \)
*   **Variance:** \( \text{Var}(R) = e^{2\mu + \sigma^2}(e^{\sigma^2} - 1) \approx \sigma^2(1 + 2\mu) \)

### 4. Advanced Tools: Characteristic Functions & Cumulants

The characteristic function is the Fourier transform of the density, used to generate all moments cleanly [23, 24]:
\[ \tilde{f}(t) \equiv \text{E}[e^{itX}] = \int_{-\infty}^{\infty} e^{itx} p(x) dx \]
\[ \text{E}[X^\ell] = (-i)^\ell \frac{d^\ell}{dt^\ell} \tilde{f}(t) \Bigg|_{t=0} \]

**Cumulant Expansion:**
Since Fourier transforms multiply when adding independent random variables, their logarithms add [24, 25]. We define cumulants \( C_n \) as:
\[ C_n = (-i)^n \frac{d^n}{dt^n} \log \tilde{f}(t) \Bigg|_{t=0} \]
*   \( C_1 = \langle X \rangle \) (Mean) [26]
*   \( C_2 = \langle X^2 \rangle - \langle X \rangle^2 \) (Variance) [26]
**Key Insight:** For a Gaussian distribution, all cumulants \( C_n \) are exactly zero for \( n > 2 \) [26].

### 5. Waiting Times (Bernoulli Trial Extensions)

When waiting for the first "success" in repeated Bernoulli trials, let waiting time \( T \) take values \( 0, 1, 2, \dots \) corresponding to \( n \) total trials to succeed.
*   **Probability:** \( \text{Prob}(T=n) = q^{n-1}p \) [27, 28]

**Derivation of Expected Waiting Time:**
To solve \( \text{E}[T] \), we use a rigorous derivative trick, interchanging the order of summation and differentiation (valid for \( q < 1 \)) [28]:
\[ \text{E}[T] = \sum_{n=0}^{\infty} n q^{n-1} p \]
\[ \text{E}[T] = p \frac{d}{dq} \left( \sum_{n=0}^{\infty} q^n \right) \]
\[ \text{E}[T] = p \frac{d}{dq} \left( \frac{1}{1-q} \right) = \frac{p}{(1-q)^2} \]
Since \( 1-q = p \), we substitute to find:
\[ \text{E}[T] = \frac{1}{p} \]
**Variance of Waiting Time:**
Using a similar generalized derivative operator trick \( [q \frac{d}{dq}]^r \frac{1}{1-q} \), the variance evaluates to [29, 30]:
\[ \text{Var}(T) = \frac{1-p}{p^2} \]
**Pitfall Flag:** Do not factor in past trials when calculating expected future waiting times. This exhibits the *Markov property* (memorylessness) — your expected waiting time from any given point forward depends only on your current state, not on the number of prior failures [31].
